# Source Verification

Use this reference while discovering and verifying companies, contacts, and public business contact routes.

## Research Company-First

1. Discover candidate companies from product, equipment, application, project, association, exhibition, directory, Maps, and industry searches.
2. Verify that each company is real, current, and part of the target value chain.
3. Identify the concrete product connection: what it makes, uses, operates, specifies, distributes, services, or procures.
4. Only then locate current relevant people or official public business contact routes.

This order prevents attractive job titles at irrelevant companies from entering the list.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order, while cross-checking when a claim affects qualification:

1. Official company website, product page, project page, tender notice, registry, or verified company account.
2. Current LinkedIn company/profile data, official association, exhibition, dealership, or government listing.
3. Google Maps business listing and reputable industry directories.
4. Reputable news, project databases, or other secondary sources.
5. Search snippets only for discovery, never as sole proof of a formal lead.

For every decisive fact, retain the fact, source URL, source type, capture date, and confidence. Keep these categories distinct:

- user-provided information;
- source text;
- verified fact supported by evidence;
- model inference and its reasoning.

## Verify The Company

Confirm as many of these as relevant:

- legal or trading name and root domain;
- country, city, service area, and operational status;
- company type and position in the value chain;
- products, equipment, applications, projects, or customers that establish fit;
- official public contact page, business phone, email, or messaging route;
- current event or strategy signals when used for ranking or personalization.

A company name alone is insufficient. Explain its relationship to at least one target product.

## Verify The Person

Use a current profile, current company page, official team page, or another traceable source. Confirm:

- full name;
- current company;
- current role and function;
- country or business coverage when relevant;
- profile/source URL;
- why this role connects to the product or buying process.

Do not treat education, likes, activity counts, or a former job as evidence of current purchasing influence. Do not infer that a person is seeking suppliers because of their title or company growth.

## Contact Routes

Only collect contact data published for business use or supplied by the user. Record where it was found.

- LinkedIn: keep the canonical profile URL and Lead ID when available.
- Email: prefer official company-domain addresses; label generic and person-specific addresses accurately.
- Phone: normalize the country code and retain the displayed source form.
- WhatsApp: normalize to E.164 only after verifying that the number is publicly presented as a business/WhatsApp contact.
- Maps: retain Place ID, full address, category, root domain, and phone when available.

Never guess an email pattern, phone number, or WhatsApp availability and present it as verified.

## Evidence Outcome

Assign one of these outcomes to each candidate:

- `verified`: decisive company and contact facts have traceable evidence.
- `company_only`: the company fits, but no current relevant person or official route is verified.
- `needs_review`: evidence conflicts or a decisive fact is unclear.
- `rejected`: outside the ICP, inactive, unverifiable, or prohibited by an exclusion.

Keep a reason for non-verified outcomes so later passes do not repeat the same failed research.
